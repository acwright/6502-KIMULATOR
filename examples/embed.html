Embedding the 6502 KIMulator

Reference: docs/EMBEDDING.md. Serving from — override with ?base=….

1. Two embeds on one page

Click into one and type: only the focused frame takes the keyboard, and inside a frame the pad and the terminal are separate focus regions with a lit keyboard badge on whichever holds it. Click the page background to give the keyboard back to this document — arrow keys and space should scroll here, not disappear into a machine.

They are also two different machines. A is the whole KIM. B is panels=lcd,keys on a machine with serialcard=0: no Serial Card in io5, no terminal to show, and the KC Monitor on the path the firmware guards behind HW_PRESENT & HW_SC — which is the machine a docs page about the pad actually wants.

A — all four panels, controls=full, LEDs fitted
B — panels=lcd,keys, serialcard=0

2. Driving a frame over postMessage

Every button below targets embed A. The log shows what comes back: 6502-kim:ready once, then 6502-kim:serial and 6502-kim:stopped as they happen. There is no lcd event — the glass is on screen, and a page that needs to read it wants the debug protocol and a real machine.

6502-kim:key presses and never releases, because the MM74C922 reports the press and nothing else. A sequence goes in one message so the embed can pace it in emulated time — the encoder latches one code, and the interrupt handler's read is what makes room for the next.

6502-kim:show does nothing while embed A is wide enough to show every panel at once — there is nothing to bring to the front. Narrow this window, or open the page on a phone, and the same buttons move the switch in its control bar.

3. Loading a binary, two ways

The same file at $0800, sent as a 6502-kim:load message (no reload) or baked into a fresh frame as bin64= (no network fetch at all — this is the form a docs site wants for inline samples, and a type-in card is a few dozen bytes). There is no cart: the Keypad Card is this machine's cartridge and it is soldered in.
